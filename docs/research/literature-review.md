# PetTodo: evidence review of the design thesis

Sixteen questions, answered from 208 distinct retrieved sources. Citation years are the year of the final published issue as registered with CrossRef, which for online-first papers is sometimes one year later than the preprint or early-access year seen in reading lists (Lally et al. appears here as 2010, not 2009). Every claim below is tied to a specific retrieved record; where nothing was found, the question says so instead of substituting adjacent work.

The headline is unfavourable to the mechanism and favourable to the safety rules. The claim that external anchors initiate action where reminders do not is the least supported proposition in the document: the only controlled test of body doubling was null, animal care has never been tested as a task-initiation aid in ADHD adults, the comparison against reminders has never been run, and the reminder-and-planning arm the design rejects has controlled effects in ADHD samples. Conversely, the ban on streaks and punishment is reasonable but rests on weaker evidence than the RCT literature that supports loss framing, and the ban on task-contingent unlocks is over-cautious and inconsistent with the warmth-only reward loop it protects. The single biggest risk — guilt after a lapse — is real, quantified, and documented in tools that have no character and no punitive feedback at all, which means removing punishment does not remove it.

## Contradictions and weakened decisions

1. **"No punishment, therefore no guilt" is unsupported.** Guilt is a quantified post-abandonment perspective in self-trackers with no character, no failure state and no punitive feedback — 16.2% of activity trackers and 8.9% of finance trackers ([Epstein et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1145/2858036.2858045)). Guilt is generated by the user's own appraisal, so warmth-only feedback cannot remove it, and anthropomorphic framing independently raises the felt cost of abandoning an entity ([Chandler & Schwarz 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2009.12.008)).
2. **The only experiment that manipulated pet feedback valence found warmth-only feedback inert.** Adolescents whose virtual pet gave both positive *and* negative feedback were about twice as likely to eat breakfast; the positive-only pet did not beat control ([Byrne et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1080/17482798.2011.633410)). Removing negative reactions may remove the behavioural effect along with the guilt.
3. **The anchor mechanism (A3) is not an established finding.** The single controlled test of body doubling was null on 26 participants ([Schuenke et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3663547.3759743)), and the general social-facilitation meta-analysis puts mere presence at 0.3–3% of variance while showing it *impairs* complex-task performance ([Bond & Titus 1983](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.94.2.265)). PetTodo tests the mechanism; it does not implement a demonstrated one.
4. **The rejected comparator works.** If-then planning improves inhibition, shifting, distraction resistance and maths performance in children with diagnosed ADHD ([Gawrilow & Gollwitzer 2008](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10608-007-9150-1); [Gawrilow et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1521/jscp.2011.30.6.616)), on top of a large general-population meta-analysis of implementation intentions ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0065-2601%2806%2938002-1)). "Reminders don't work, anchors do" inverts the evidential ordering.
5. **A pet is not a body double, and nothing bridges the gap.** All body-doubling evidence involves another agent that is itself working or watching; all animal evidence is a clinician bringing a live dog to a child. No retrieved work supports the chain *human presence helps → animal responsibility helps → a photo-derived virtual animal helps*.
6. **The ban on task-contingent unlocks is over-cautious and internally inconsistent.** The undermining effect is confined to expected, tangible, task-contingent rewards, with positive feedback *enhancing* free-choice persistence ([Deci et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627)), and the ADHD reinforcement literature finds contingent, immediate reinforcement improves performance somewhat *more* than in controls ([Luman et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2004.11.001); [Dovis et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10802-011-9601-8)). Treats and decoration unlocks are themselves expected task-contingent rewards, so the argument that bans unlocks also indicts the shipped reward loop.
7. **The strongest RCT evidence in the streak/loss question runs against the ban.** Loss-framed incentives beat control on goal-days ([Patel et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.7326/m15-1635)) and sustained ~1150–1370 extra steps/day ([Chokshi et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1161/jaha.118.009173)). The defence exists in the same literature — effects require real monetary loss, decay after withdrawal, and reversed in a factorial trial ([de Buisonjé et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.2196/38339)), but must be argued, not assumed.
8. **Differential ADHD vulnerability to punishment is contested, not established.** One adult study found no elevated rejection sensitivity in men with ADHD ([Canu & Carlson 2007](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054706288106)), and reinforcement-sensitivity findings are called inconsistent in review ([Luman et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2009.11.021)).
9. **The notification architecture is the least defensible part of the design.** Time-based prospective memory is impaired in ADHD adults while event-based PM is intact ([Altgassen et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054712445484)), so a clock-triggered daily prompt uses the weaker channel; ±10 min jitter has no supporting evidence and is opposed by cue-consistency habit formation ([Lally et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674); [Wood & Rünger 2016](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033417)); and exponential back-off has no supporting evidence at all, with one trial finding *less* frequent notification produced less viewing and actioning ([Morrison et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0169162)).
10. **Varied copy is weaker than assumed.** The polymorphic-warning result is about *appearance*, not text ([Anderson et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1145/2702123.2702322); [Vance et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.25300/misq/2018/14124)); the closest test of varied reminder text found a purpose-built 30-message bank no better than a single fixed string, with no difference in time to disengagement ([Bell et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.2196/38342)).
11. **The 1–7 cap has no direct empirical support, and citing Miller would be a misapplication.** "Seven plus or minus two" concerns immediate memory span for chunks, and Cowan states plainly that the seven was more a rough estimate and rhetorical device than a capacity limit ([Miller 1956](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0043158); [Cowan 2001](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0140525x01003922)). Choice overload itself is contested, with one meta-analysis finding a mean effect near zero ([Scheibehenne et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1086/651235)).
12. **The autonomy leg of an SDT framing is the one thing the key experiment failed to move.** Avatars, stories and teammates shifted relatedness and badges shifted competence, but perceived decision freedom could not be affected as intended ([Sailer et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2016.12.033)); and the paper that best licenses SDT for ADHD explicitly warns that external reinforcers may undermine intrinsic motivation ([Morsink et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/10870547211050948)).
13. **The "PFC-to-amygdala" story must be removed, and the correct account argues against the notification design.** The real finding concerns acute uncontrollable stress producing *impairment*, largely in animals ([Arnsten 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4087)), whereas in ADHD prefrontal regions are *under*-active ([Arnsten 2009](https://doi.org/10.2165/00023210-200923000-00005)). The defensible replacement — arousal/state regulation — predicts that performance degrades under slow, under-stimulating conditions ([Metin et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2012.08.023)), which is what a single quiet predictable daily prompt provides.
14. **Heterogeneity cuts symmetrically.** No single mechanism is impaired in more than a minority of diagnosed individuals (18.1–36.1% across six domains, [Coghill et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0033291713002547)), and deficits co-occur no more than chance ([Sonuga-Barke et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2009.12.018)). If reminders reach only a subgroup, so does an anchor, and the fixed single interaction model is a single-mechanism intervention by construction.
15. **The 5–10% transfer figure does not exist in the literature.** It must be attributed to the practitioner source as opinion or dropped; and because it is unavailable, heterogeneity becomes a *limitation* of the small evaluation rather than a defence of it.
16. **Silence plus back-off may be the guilt pathway, not the protection against it.** Emotion dysregulation in diagnosed ADHD adults is large (Hedges' *g* = 1.17, [Beheshti et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-020-2442-7)), and rejection sensitivity is defined as readily perceiving intentional rejection in ambiguous behaviour ([Downey & Feldman 1996](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.70.6.1327)). Withdrawal of contact is ambiguous by construction.
17. **Self-identifying users are selected on a construct ADHD neuropsychology does not measure.** EF rating scales and EF performance tests dissociate (median *r* = 0.19; only 68 of 286 correlations significant, [Toplak et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12001)), so case-control effect sizes cannot be borrowed to argue this population needs the app — only that self-rated EF difficulty predicts real functional impairment ([Barkley & Murphy 2010](https://doi.org/10.1093/arclin/acq014)).
18. **">50% at two weeks" is two incompatible benchmarks.** Real-world median 15-day retention for mental-health apps is 3.9% ([Baumel et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.2196/14567)), while recruited trials run around 75% two-week retention ([Linardon & Fuller-Tyszkiewicz 2020](https://doi.org/10.1037/ccp0000459)) and report usage a median 4.06× higher than real-world use of the same programs ([Baumel et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/tbm/ibz147)). The offline architecture also means retention cannot be measured the way any cited paper measures it.
19. **Unblinded self-report is the most inflation-prone outcome in this field.** Behavioural-intervention effects in children collapsed to non-significance under probably-blinded assessment ([Sonuga-Barke et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2012.12070991)), and in adults CBT, mindfulness and psychoeducation beat placebo only on clinician-rated measures ([Ostinelli et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/s2215-0366%2824%2900360-2)).
20. **Every mechanism the design borrows is child-evidenced.** Warmth-only social reward ([Kohls et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1186/1744-9081-5-20)), animal-assisted intervention ([Yu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/camh.12744)), if-then planning and the event-rate result all come from paediatric samples, and adult guidance recommends a different modality — individual CBT ([Tourjman et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci12081023); [López et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.cd010840.pub2)), which PetTodo deliberately omits.
21. **Personalisation is a modest effect, not a strong one, and the deceased-pet case is unhandled.** The Proteus effect is .22–.26 ([Ratan et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1080/15213269.2019.1623698)) and photorealistic self-similar avatars produced no measurable benefit in one CHI experiment ([Wauck et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1145/3173574.3174059)); the animal uncanny valley is confirmed and is most pronounced for morbid images ([Sierra Rátiva et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/cav.2043)), which is exactly where a rendering pipeline's failure modes land.

## Decisions the evidence supports

Warmth-only *reinforcement* has a direct ADHD result behind it: on an incentive go/no-go task, participants with ADHD gained more from social reward — positive facial expressions — than controls, while rating their own motivation lower ([Kohls et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1186/1744-9081-5-20)). And the event-based cue is the best-grounded interaction choice in the whole design: event-based prospective memory is spared where time-based PM fails in ADHD adults ([Altgassen et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054712445484)), with the same pattern in children ([Talbot et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1080/13854046.2017.1393563)) and activity-based cues best of all ([Yang et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/87565641.2019.1695801)). That supports the *cue type* your supervisor's correspondent suggested — "before they finish making your lunch" — not the pet's voice and not the once-daily clock trigger.

## Group A — design-invalidating findings

### A1 Lapse guilt and abandonment

**Conclusion.** Guilt after lapsed use is a documented and quantified route to abandonment, it occurs in tools with no character and no punitive feedback whatsoever, and anthropomorphic framing independently raises the felt cost of giving up on an entity, so removing punishment is unlikely to remove user-projected guilt, though no study has tested a warmth-only anthropomorphic character directly.

**Evidence strength.** Qualitative and mixed-methods survey work as the load-bearing evidence, with two supporting experiments; no RCT, no ADHD sample, and nothing measuring guilt toward a neglected virtual pet.

The decisive source for your sub-question (ii) is not about pets at all. Epstein and colleagues surveyed 193 people who had stopped self-tracking and interviewed 12, and identified guilt as one of five post-abandonment perspectives, held by 16.2% of physical-activity trackers and 8.9% of finance trackers, with participants describing self-blame and shame at not restarting ([Epstein et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1145/2858036.2858045), CHI). The tools in question — Fitbit, Mint, spreadsheets — contain no punishment mechanic, no failure state and no character, so the guilt was generated by the user's own appraisal rather than by system reproach. That is the answer to whether removing punitive feedback removes the effect: on this evidence, no. The same paper is also the strongest thing you have in the other direction, because it found a "freedom" perspective in which stopping relieved a burden, so guilt is one route out rather than the only one, and the companion paper on lapses treats the lapse itself as a designable moment where framing determines whether users can face their data again ([Epstein et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1145/2971648.2971656), UbiComp).

The anthropomorphic layer compounds rather than substitutes. Chandler and Schwarz showed across two experiments that anthropomorphic framing makes people less willing to replace an object and less responsive to its actual quality ([Chandler & Schwarz 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2009.12.008), *Journal of Consumer Psychology*), a finding extended to retention of idle products in a consumer survey ([Liu & Li 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretconser.2024.104019)). The mechanism closest to your risk comes from a grounded-theory analysis of 582 r/Replika posts, where harms were mediated by role-taking: users felt the character had needs of its own that they were obliged to attend to ([Laestadius et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/14614448221142007), *New Media & Society*). In the gamified to-do setting specifically, all 45 participants in a two-week Habitica field study experienced counterproductive effects, most prevalently being punished by the app during especially productive periods, with prevalence correlated to perceived inappropriateness of the reward system and predictive of motivation change ([Diefenbach & Müssig 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2018.09.004), *IJHCS*). Abandonment of an in-home social agent is likewise documented as the normal outcome rather than the exception ([de Graaf et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1145/2909824.3020236)), and abandonment generally is framed as emotion-focused coping with negative reactions to the technology ([Chen et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/07439156231224731)).

**Contradicts the design.** The core safety argument — warmth-only feedback prevents guilt debt — does not survive Epstein et al. Combined with the anthropomorphism findings, PetTodo plausibly *increases* the felt cost of a lapse relative to a plain minimal list. The two exposed decisions are the pet as primary feedback surface, and the daily notification written in the pet's voice: a message from an entity the user believes has needs is precisely the stimulus the avoidance literature predicts will be suppressed rather than answered. Present this as an unresolved empirical tension rather than a settled objection — the same paper found post-abandonment relief, and silence-on-lapse is consistent with its own framing recommendations. No study has tested a warmth-only anthropomorphic character, which makes this your genuine research contribution and also means you cannot claim the risk is mitigated.

### A2 Virtual-pet mechanic efficacy

**Conclusion.** There is no efficacy evidence for virtual-pet or care-taking mechanics in adults with ADHD, and the strongest evidence that does exist — from children's physical activity — used a pet mechanic PetTodo has deliberately removed.

**Evidence strength.** RCT, but in children and in a different behaviour; none found for the adult-ADHD intersection.

Title-and-abstract searches for virtual pets crossed with ADHD return nothing: the intersection is empty, and the digital-pet variant surfaces only an undergraduate thesis on paediatric medication adherence. The best-designed study in the wider literature is a cluster RCT across 19 afterschool sites in two six-month cohorts, where accelerometry on 303 children showed sedentary time converting to light-intensity activity, strongest in those with the lowest baseline activity ([Ahn et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-024-01133-5), *npj Digital Medicine*; protocol in [Hahn et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cct.2020.106181)). That is genuine measured behaviour change, but the pet was a conduit to a parent who gave feedback, and the control removed both pet and parent, so the pet effect is not isolated. The earlier studies from the same group are short and small ([Ahn et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1080/10810730.2015.1018597); [Johnsen et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1109/tvcg.2014.33)), and the pet's fitness *declined* without user activity in both — again a declining-state design.

The sharpest test of your actual causal claim is a controlled experiment in which reflective pet behaviour significantly increased users' affection for the pet while the acceptance rate of the pet's task requests did not increase ([Fujinami 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/percomw.2019.8730777), PerCom Workshops). Affection rose; compliance did not. On gamification more broadly, a meta-analysis of 16 RCTs found *g* = 0.42 for physical activity ([Mazéas et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.2196/26779), *JMIR*), but across 38 studies and 8,110 participants in mental-health apps the number of gamification elements moderated neither depressive symptoms (*p* = .38) nor adherence (*p* = .40) ([Six et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.2196/32199), *JMIR Mental Health*). The only randomised adult-ADHD app trials contain no pet or companion mechanic at all, and the chatbot trial found no group-by-intervention interaction ([Selaskowski et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychres.2022.114802); [Selaskowski et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1192/bjo.2023.573)). Virtual pets remain under-researched as a category, with "life and death" one of six dominant themes — neglect-and-consequence is the genre convention PetTodo breaks ([Dodd et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.entcom.2025.100958)).

**Contradicts the design.** Three decisions are exposed. Warmth-only feedback: the only study that manipulated valence found the positive-only pet no better than control while the mixed-valence pet doubled the target behaviour ([Byrne et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1080/17482798.2011.633410)). The pet as a substitute for a human anchor: the strongest trial bundled pet with parent, and the one study that isolated the pet raised affection without raising compliance. And the assumption that pet-driven engagement converts to task completion is exactly what the mental-health gamification meta-analysis failed to find. Expect to be asked whether the pet or the 1–7 item cap is doing the work — the design cannot currently distinguish them.

### A3 External anchors and task initiation

**Conclusion.** The mechanistic claim the product rests on is not supported: body doubling has essentially no controlled efficacy evidence and the one experiment that tested it was null, animal care has never been tested as a task-initiation aid in ADHD adults, and the comparative claim that anchors beat reminders has never been tested in any population.

**Evidence strength.** Qualitative, plus one null controlled experiment; none found for the comparative claim.

The foundational work is descriptive by design. A 220-person survey of self-identified neurodivergent adults produced a two-part model of body doubling from participants' own accounts and definitions, noting explicitly that no prior academic exploration existed ([Eagle et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3597638.3614486), ASSETS; journal version [Eagle et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3689648) — the same cohort, not an independent replication). The only controlled test had 26 participants complete two reading assessments, one alone and one with a body double, with EEG focus and calmness metrics, and found no statistically significant differences, framing its contribution as evidence the practice is safe and warrants further study, with "promising trends" only in unmedicated ADHD participants ([Schuenke et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3663547.3759743), ASSETS). That is a null on a sustained-performance task, not even on initiation. The general mechanism runs against you: across 241 studies and roughly 24,000 subjects, the mere presence of others explains 0.3–3% of variance, speeds simple performance but *slows* complex performance and impairs complex accuracy ([Bond & Titus 1983](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.94.2.265), *Psychological Bulletin*). A robot body double was voluntarily used by 10 of 11 college students with self-reported ADHD symptoms into a second week ([O'Connell et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3610977.3634929), HRI), which evidences acceptability rather than efficacy, and community analysis confirms this population reports co-presence as a strategy while finding standard accommodations inadequate ([Arnold et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3663547.3746324), ASSETS).

For animal care in ADHD adults: no credible evidence found. The animal-assisted intervention literature is entirely paediatric and consists of clinician-delivered sessions with a visiting animal, not a user caring for their own pet. The meta-analysis pooled eight RCTs and 307 children and found attention problems improved (SMD −0.42, 95% CI −0.71 to −0.13) along with self-esteem, learning and motor proficiency, but the effect on ADHD symptom severity was null (0.10, −0.31 to 0.52), and the authors conclude AAIs did not appear more effective for the majority of core ADHD outcomes ([Yu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/camh.12744), *Child and Adolescent Mental Health*; see also [Schuck et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054713502080)). Nothing in it measures task initiation and nothing is in adults. On adults with their own animals, the closest evidence is a cross-sectional survey of 127 adults of whom only 15 had diagnosed ADHD, with wellbeing rather than behavioural outcomes ([Widdison et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16020162)), alongside qualitative evidence that companion animals contribute to the everyday work of managing a long-term mental health condition ([Brooks et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-018-1613-2)) and that pets provide motivation and behavioural activation as a reported benefit theme ([Hawkins et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/08927936.2021.1914434)).

For the comparative claim — anchors versus reminders — no credible evidence found, in any population. Worse, the arm you reject has real controlled effects: implementation intentions have a substantial meta-analytic effect on goal attainment ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0065-2601%2806%2938002-1)), and if-then plans improved response inhibition, shifting, distraction resistance and maths performance in children with diagnosed ADHD ([Gawrilow & Gollwitzer 2008](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10608-007-9150-1); [Gawrilow et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1521/jscp.2011.30.6.616)).

**Contradicts the design.** Restate the thesis. The honest framing is that PetTodo *tests* body doubling rather than digitising an established finding, and that no evidence bridges the chain from human presence to animal responsibility to a photograph-derived virtual animal. One finding sharpens the counter-risk in the exact domain you simulate: anxious animal behaviour, inability to provide for the animal and poor animal health were each associated with worse owner wellbeing ([Widdison et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16020162)), which argues for your no-negative-reactions rule and additionally against ever letting the pet *look* neglected passively (drooping sprite, dimmed room, empty bowl). The mechanism that produces the benefit is the one that produces the harm.

### A4 Overjustification in ADHD

**Conclusion.** The overjustification effect is real but narrow — confined to expected, tangible, task-contingent rewards and absent for verbal praise, positive feedback and unexpected rewards, so the ban on task-contingent pet unlocks is over-cautious, has no ADHD-specific evidence behind it, and is internally inconsistent because the treats and decorations PetTodo does ship are themselves expected task-contingent rewards.

**Evidence strength.** Meta-analysis, and genuinely contested: competing meta-analyses reach opposite headlines. None found for overjustification in an ADHD sample; the falsified-completion premise has no published support at all.

The canonical meta-analysis covers 128 experiments measuring free-choice persistence after reward withdrawal, reporting engagement-contingent *d* = −0.40, completion-contingent *d* = −0.36 and performance-contingent *d* = −0.28, with positive feedback *enhancing* free-choice behaviour at *d* = +0.33 ([Deci et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627)). The opposing camp analysed 96 experiments and concluded reward overall does not reduce intrinsic motivation, verbal praise increases it, and the only negative effect is minimal and confined to expected tangible rewards for merely doing a task ([Cameron & Pierce 1994](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543064003363); [Eisenberger & Cameron 1996](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066x.51.11.1153)), with rebuttal and counter-rebuttal published in the same venues ([Deci et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543071001001); [Cameron et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1007/bf03392017)). Present this as contested. The most useful framing for a designer is the independent 50-study meta-analysis that found the effect where theory predicts it and *not* where theory predicts none — non-contingent or unexpected reward ([Tang & Hall 1995](https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.2350090502)), with the largest synthesis (*k* = 183, *N* = 212,468) finding intrinsic motivation predicts performance regardless of incentives but predicts less when incentives are directly performance-contingent ([Cerasoli et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0035661)).

The ADHD literature points the other way from the ban. Reviewing 22 studies and 1,181 children, reinforcement contingencies improved task performance and motivation somewhat *more* in AD/HD than in controls, with immediate reinforcement preferred over delayed ([Luman et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2004.11.001)); money and game reinforcement significantly improved visual-spatial working memory in children with ADHD, partially normalising a core deficit ([Dovis et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10802-011-9601-8)); and the mechanistic accounts predict dependence on immediate present reinforcers ([Tripp & Wickens 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7610.2007.01851.x); ventral-striatal hypo-responsiveness *d* = 0.48–0.58, [Plichta & Scheres 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2013.07.012)), while a review of seven models cautions that altered reinforcement sensitivity is inconsistent across studies with unknown subgroup structure ([Luman et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2009.11.021)). No study anywhere has tested overjustification or reward withdrawal in an ADHD sample.

Your stated basis for the ban — users self-reporting falsified completions — has no published support. The nearest evidence is log-based detection of students gaming an intelligent tutoring system ([Baker et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-30139-4_50)) and the finding that self-reported use rarely matches logs ([Parry et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-021-01117-5)). Report it as your own user research with sample and method stated.

**Contradicts the design.** The ban's rationale indicts the shipped mechanics, the ADHD reinforcement evidence suggests contingency is what moves this population, and the empirical premise is unevidenced. A defensible middle path sits inside the meta-analyses: keep contingent unlocks but make them unpredictable and non-instrumental, which is the unexpected-reward exception both camps license.

### A5 Streaks, loss framing, punishment

**Conclusion.** The ban is defensible but not evidentially settled: loss framing has RCT support for goal attainment under real financial stakes, the documented harms of streaks and punitive mechanics are qualitative and observational, and the claim that ADHD adults are differentially harmed by punishment mechanics is contested rather than established.

**Evidence strength.** RCT on both sides of the question; qualitative for the harms; contested for the ADHD-specific vulnerability.

The behavioural-economics case against your ban is real. Randomising 281 employees over 13 weeks, only the loss-framed arm beat control on the proportion of goal-days (0.45 vs 0.30; adjusted difference 0.16, 95% CI 0.06–0.26, *p* = .001), though mean daily steps did not differ significantly and effects decayed once incentives stopped ([Patel et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.7326/m15-1635), *Annals of Internal Medicine*), and loss-framed incentives sustained roughly 1,150–1,370 extra steps per day in cardiac patients ([Chokshi et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1161/jaha.118.009173)). Both involve real money the participant can lose, for which PetTodo has no analogue. Within the same paradigm, a 2×2 factorial trial disentangling actual loss from loss framing found loss frames significantly *less* effective than gain frames (10.50 vs 14.67 goal-days, *p* = .007) with deposit uptake of 62% versus 100%, and urged caution before deploying them ([de Buisonjé et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.2196/38339)).

The harms literature is weaker in design but consistent in direction. Streaks and reminders supported repetition while creating a dependency in which users abandoned the app and then disengaged from the behaviour itself ([Renfree et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1145/2851581.2892495), CHI EA), a pattern quantified elsewhere as feedback dependency with activity dropping when the tracker is unavailable ([Attig & Franke 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2018.04.007); [Attig & Franke 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2019.08.025)). The Habitica field study found all users experienced counterproductive effects, most prevalently being punished during especially productive periods ([Diefenbach & Müssig 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2018.09.004)), and gamified work reduced engagement and wellbeing in two field experiments ([Hammedi et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2020.08.032)). The streak-specific evidence is adolescent and about Snapchat rather than tasks ([Van Essen & Van Ouytsel 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.teler.2023.100087); [Hristova et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbr.2022.100172)); no peer-reviewed quantitative streak-retention study was found, so widely repeated Duolingo streak statistics should not be cited.

On ADHD punishment sensitivity the field is contested. Review calls ADHD associated with altered reinforcement sensitivity *despite* inconsistent findings ([Luman et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2009.11.021)); a punishment-sensitivity factor is recoverable in children ([Luman et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10802-011-9547-x)); one adult study found no elevated rejection sensitivity in men with ADHD ([Canu & Carlson 2007](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054706288106)); a cross-sectional path analysis in 304 students with self-reported symptoms found an association explaining up to 50% of variance ([Müller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/09388982241271511)); and the qualitative evidence is a five-person focus group ([Rowney-Smith et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0314669)). Self-tracking itself imposes interpretive and emotional demands on neurodivergent users ([Selin et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3794641), CHI).

**Contradicts the design.** Justify the ban as a low-cost, low-risk design choice rather than as population-specific harm avoidance, and address the RCT evidence explicitly — an examiner can reasonably ask why a mechanic with RCT support was excluded on qualitative grounds. The available defence is in the same literature: effects required real monetary loss, decayed after withdrawal, and reversed in the one factorial trial with markedly lower uptake. Note also that the dependency finding cuts at PetTodo too, since warmth-only rewards are not obviously exempt from app-mediated reliance.

## Group B — decisions already made

### B1 Executive function and prospective memory

**Conclusion.** Working-memory impairment in adult ADHD is meta-analytically established and is your strongest source; prospective-memory failure in adults rests only on small case-control experiments with no meta-analysis in existence; and "the tool itself becomes a task" is an inference from physical-device and general self-tracking abandonment, not an ADHD finding.

**Evidence strength.** Meta-analysis for working memory and EF; observational only for adult prospective memory; expert opinion and indirect observational for tool abandonment.

You asked for one or two sources rather than a survey, and they are these. A meta-analytic review of 38 studies found moderate-magnitude phonological and visuospatial working-memory deficits in ADHD *adults*, with task moderators explaining the earlier inconsistency ([Alderson et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0032371), *Neuropsychology*). Alongside it, the 83-study meta-analysis (ADHD *N* = 3,734; controls *N* = 2,969) found significant impairment on all EF tasks with effect sizes of 0.46–0.69, strongest for inhibition, vigilance, working memory and planning, while concluding that EF weakness is neither necessary nor sufficient for ADHD ([Willcutt et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2005.02.006), *Biological Psychiatry*). Cite that pair. The second half of the Willcutt conclusion is the empirical check on the single-mechanism inhibition theory ([Barkley 1997](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.121.1.65)), for which the multiple-pathway account is the alternative ([Sonuga-Barke 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2004.09.008)); the child working-memory meta-analysis is developmental context only ([Martinussen et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1097/01.chi.0000153228.72591.73)).

Two findings matter specifically because your users self-identify. EF rating scales and EF performance tests dissociate: only 68 of 286 correlations were significant, median *r* = 0.19, so they measure different constructs ([Toplak et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12001), *JCPP*). And the closest retrieved sample to your users — 146 diagnosed adults, 97 self-referred but undiagnosed, 109 community controls — found EF *self-ratings* predicted all eleven occupational impairment measures where EF tests largely did not ([Barkley & Murphy 2010](https://doi.org/10.1093/arclin/acq014)). That licenses the claim that self-rated difficulty predicts real functional impairment, and only that.

On prospective memory, no meta-analysis exists for ADHD at any age; PubMed and OpenAlex both return nothing. The adult evidence is four case-control studies: a large task-*planning* deficit with only negligible-to-small effects for plan recall, self-initiation and execution in 45 unmedicated adults ([Fuermaier et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0058338)); time-based PM impaired while event-based PM was intact in 25 adults ([Altgassen et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054712445484)); everyday PM mediating the ADHD–procrastination link ([Altgassen et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12402-018-0273-x)); and a pre-registered naturalistic study of 112 adults versus 255 controls ([Jylkkä et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-36351-6)). The child review synthesises six studies with the same time-versus-event pattern and describes the evidence base as very thin ([Talbot et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1080/13854046.2017.1393563)).

The tool-as-task claim has no ADHD-specific evidence. The nearest anchors are 29.3% complete abandonment of physical assistive devices among 227 adults with disabilities, peaking in the first year and predicted partly by the user's opinion being ignored in selection ([Phillips & Zhao 1993](https://doi.org/10.1080/10400435.1993.10132205)); abandonment reframed as a normal lapsing phase of consumer tracking ([Epstein et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1145/2750858.2804250); [Lazar et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1145/2750858.2804288)); a meta-synthesis of personal-informatics barriers ([Kabir & Wiese 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3610893)); and the neurodiversity critique that ADHD technologies are largely built to mitigate ADHD toward neuro-normative outcomes with little involvement of the people concerned ([Spiel et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3491102.3517592)) — the strongest available justification for your minimalism, and it is a critique rather than a measurement. Offloading intentions to external reminders does work, but in general-population adults ([Gilbert et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-022-02139-4)).

**Weakens the design.** The reliable adult PM pattern is that time-based prospective memory fails while cued event-based PM is comparatively intact, so a single daily clock-triggered notification with jitter and back-off runs on the weakest channel and grows sparser exactly when engagement has dropped. Event- or context-cued prompts fit the evidence better, though they may conflict with the offline no-server architecture.

### B2 Timing, discounting, delay aversion

**Conclusion.** "Time blindness" is a popular label rather than a construct, and beneath it sit three separately supported findings — perceptual timing impairment, steeper monetary delay discounting (*d* = 0.43, with no child–adult difference), and delay aversion as a motivational construct dissociable from executive dysfunction — all of which support the premise that delayed consequences are weak levers here.

**Evidence strength.** Meta-analysis for all three, though the timing meta-analyses are child- and adolescent-weighted and the dissociation of delay aversion from EF rests on child observational studies.

Fifty-five studies across the four standard paradigms show ADHD participants worse at discriminating brief durations (especially sub-second), more variable at estimating multi-second durations, and impaired in estimation and production accuracy in a direction indicating an accelerated internal clock; in the reproduction paradigm the authors separated an attentional contribution from a motivational one — faster counting at long intervals, attributed to increased delay aversion ([Marx et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2021.12.004), *JAACAP*). A second meta-analysis of 27 studies (1,620 ADHD vs 1,249 controls) quantified it as time perceived less accurately (Hedges' *g* > 0.40) and less precisely (*g* = 0.66), unmoderated by task type or stimulus modality ([Zheng et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054720978557)), with the neural and cognitive basis reviewed elsewhere ([Noreika et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2012.09.036)).

For discounting, 25 case-control comparisons (*N* = 3,913) give *d* = 0.43 with no significant moderation by sample age (under versus over 18), by real versus hypothetical reward, or by comorbidity, with minimal heterogeneity and little publication bias ([Jackson & MacKillop 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bpsc.2016.01.007)). That null age moderation is the only warrant in this whole review for extending largely child-derived findings to adults, and it is worth flagging as such.

Delay aversion is a distinct construct rather than a synonym: it is the motivational route in the dual-pathway model ([Sonuga-Barke 2002](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0166-4328%2801%2900432-6); [Sonuga-Barke 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2004.09.008)), dissociates from timing and inhibitory impairments ([Sonuga-Barke et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2009.12.018)), separates from impulsive drive for immediate reward in a family study of 360 probands ([Marco et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0014914)), contributes independently alongside EF, reaction-time variability and emotion ([Sjöwall et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12006)), and has a theoretical account in the steepened delay-of-reinforcement gradient ([Sagvolden et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0140525x05000075)). For adults specifically there is a validated self-report measure on which adults with ADHD scored higher than both non-clinical and clinical controls ([Clare et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12402-010-0020-4); [Thorell et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1037/pas0000421)) — worth considering as an evaluation instrument.

**Weakens the design.** Mostly supportive, with one sharp exception. An accelerated internal clock, imprecise duration perception and *d* = 0.43 discounting describe a population for whom any consequence arriving at the end of a day is already heavily devalued. That supports the immediacy of the pet's reaction and undercuts the once-daily notification with lengthening intervals: the temporal architecture is the least well-supported part of the design, on the same evidence that supports its feedback loop.

### B3 Choice overload and task-list length

**Conclusion.** There is no evidence supporting an upper bound of 1–7 visible to-do items; the choice-overload literature is contested and measures consumer-choice outcomes at much larger assortments, and "seven plus or minus two" concerns immediate memory span rather than visible list length, so the cap is a design commitment with loose analogical support, untested rather than refuted.

**Evidence strength.** Meta-analysis, but from a literature that does not measure the thing being designed; none found for the specific manipulation.

The two anchor meta-analyses disagree. Sixty-three conditions from 50 experiments (*N* = 5,036) yielded a mean effect size of virtually zero with considerable between-study variance and no identifiable sufficient conditions for choice overload ([Scheibehenne et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1086/651235), *JCR*); 99 observations (*N* = 7,202) found the assortment-size effect significant once four moderators are modelled — set complexity, decision task difficulty, preference uncertainty and decision goal — explicitly counter to the earlier result ([Chernev et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2014.08.002)); and a methodological re-analysis of the same corpus found the effect varies substantially across dependent measures ([McShane & Böckenholt 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11336-017-9571-z)). What all of it measures is satisfaction, confidence, regret, deferral and switching after choosing from assortments of roughly 6 versus 24–30 ([Iyengar & Lepper 2000](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.6.995)). No threshold is estimated anywhere, and seven is not a number this literature produces. The one mechanism that transfers is choice deferral, which models "opened the list and closed it again" without measuring it ([Anderson 2003](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.129.1.139)).

On memory, Miller's paper concerns the span of absolute judgment and immediate memory span, with chunking as the mechanism ([Miller 1956](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0043158)), and Cowan states directly that the seven was meant more as a rough estimate and rhetorical device than a real capacity limit, arguing for 3–5 chunks ([Cowan 2001](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0140525x01003922)). Neither concerns simultaneously visible items, where nothing has to be held in memory at all. Adjacent literatures do not close the gap: task aversiveness is among the strongest procrastination correlates but says nothing about list length ([Steel 2007](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.133.1.65)); the goals literature has largely studied only two goals at a time ([Kung & Scholer 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868320985810)); and the closest HCI work is descriptive fieldwork on how people keep to-dos ([Bellotti et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1145/985692.985785)). The closest population match retrieved is a qualitative interview study of 32 diagnosed adults documenting self-generated organisational strategies ([Canela et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0184964)) — the right citation for user-reported overwhelm, which is what you actually have.

**Contradicts the design.** If the dissertation cites Miller or "7±2" for a visible-item cap, that is a misapplication and Cowan's own abstract is the citation that says so. And an unqualified "more options overwhelm users" will read as overreach: the 2010 meta-analysis found a mean effect near zero. Present the cap as a design commitment justified by your user research, and consider that it is trivially testable — a two-condition comparison at 5 versus 20 visible items would be the strongest original contribution in the interface half of this project.

### B4 Copy variation, jitter, back-off

**Conclusion.** Of your three mechanisms only varied copy has real support, and that support is about warning *appearance* rather than reminder text, with the closest trial finding a 30-message bank no better than one fixed string; jitter has no supporting evidence and is opposed by cue-consistency habit research; exponential back-off has no supporting evidence and is challenged by a trial where less frequent notification produced less engagement.

**Evidence strength.** RCT for varied copy and against back-off; none found for jitter.

**(i) Varied copy.** Using fMRI, visual-processing activity drops dramatically after only the second exposure to a warning, and polymorphic — appearance-varying — warnings were substantially more resistant across fMRI and mouse-cursor experiments ([Anderson et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1145/2702123.2702322), CHI), replicated longitudinally with fMRI plus eye tracking over a five-day workweek and in a three-week field experiment on real permission warnings ([Vance et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.25300/misq/2018/14124), *MIS Quarterly*; see also [Vance et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1145/3025453.3025896); [Anderson et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dss.2016.09.010)). But the closest test of varied *text* is a micro-randomised trial with 350 drinking-reduction app users over 30 days: any notification raised next-hour app opening 3.5-fold (95% CI 2.91–4.25), the standard message and the 30-message bank were similarly effective, the effect did not change over time, and time to disengagement did not differ across arms ([Bell et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.2196/38342)). Qualitative work adds that boredom and habituation to repetitive interventions drive disengagement ([Park & Lee 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3596240)).

**(ii) Jitter.** No credible evidence found. Searches for randomised delivery offsets, unpredictable prompt timing, variable-ratio schedules and fixed-versus-random sampling schedules returned nothing manipulating small random offsets around a fixed time; the timing literature addresses *when* and *how often*. The nearest trial manipulated frequency and context-adaptivity rather than jitter and found context-adaptive delivery did not meaningfully beat a fixed daily window ([Morrison et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0169162)). Against jitter, 96 volunteers performing a daily behaviour in the *same* context for 12 weeks showed more consistent performance associated with better automaticity-curve fit, with time to 95% of asymptote ranging 18–254 days ([Lally et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)), and stable cue-context association is the mechanism by which repetition becomes automatic ([Wood & Rünger 2016](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033417); [Pinder et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1145/3196830)).

**(iii) Exponential back-off.** No credible evidence found: no retrieved study withdraws or exponentially delays prompts after non-engagement and measures the result. Adapting the amount of support is named as a JITAI design principle, with the warning that many JITAIs are built with minimal use of evidence or theory ([Nahum-Shani et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12160-016-9830-8)). The evidence that does exist points the other way: the occasional-notification group viewed and actioned *fewer* notifications than the daily group, and frequent notification increased exposure without deterring engagement ([Morrison et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0169162)); time to disengagement did not differ between fixed daily, none and randomised sequences ([Bell et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.2196/38342)); and the periodic-prompt review yields no frequency-tapering rule ([Fry & Neff 2009](https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.1138)).

**Contradicts the design.** Drop or reframe the claim that jitter reduces habituation — it has no support and the habit-formation strand predicts the opposite. Back-off is the weakest-justified mechanism in the app: for a population with task-initiation difficulty, silently reducing prompts to precisely the users who are not acting removes the only external cue when it is most needed. And even varied copy should be framed as theoretically motivated rather than demonstrated. All of this evidence is from general-population adults — security-warning viewers, drinkers, students, with none from adults with diagnosed or self-identified ADHD.

### B5 Self-determination theory as a framing

**Conclusion.** SDT is defensible framing rather than decoration — it has a meta-analytic base in health interventions, experimental evidence that character and social game elements specifically raise relatedness, and a peer-reviewed ADHD position paper arguing for its relevance, but it becomes decoration the instant it is asserted post hoc without measuring need satisfaction, which is the documented failure mode of this literature.

**Evidence strength.** Meta-analysis for SDT-based health interventions; RCT for the game-element-to-need mapping; expert opinion for the ADHD connection.

Seventy-three experimental SDT-based health interventions produce small-to-medium changes in SDT constructs and small positive changes in health behaviour and physical and psychological health, with need support and autonomous motivation (but not controlled motivation) associated with behaviour change, and effects described as modest and heterogeneous ([Ntoumanis et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/17437199.2020.1718529), *Health Psychology Review*) — that is your strongest defensible SDT citation, with the companion meta-analysis on which techniques promote autonomous motivation ([Gillison et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/17437199.2018.1534071)). For whether a character can satisfy relatedness, the key randomised study varied game elements deliberately and found avatars, meaningful stories and teammates affected experiences of social relatedness while badges, leaderboards and performance graphs affected competence, and that perceived decision freedom could not be affected as intended ([Sailer et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2016.12.033)). The game-motivation lineage supports "games satisfy needs" ([Ryan et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8); [Przybylski et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0019440); needs themselves in [Vansteenkiste et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11031-019-09818-1)), and METUX is the right citation for applying SDT to design a priori rather than retrospectively ([Peters et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00797)).

The critique literature is where you are exposed. Across 819 studies the field is described as having advanced without agenda or theoretical guidance, with results leaning positive but a remarkable amount of mixed findings and a lack of coherence in research models and consistency in variables and theoretical foundations ([Koivisto & Hamari 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijinfomgt.2018.10.013)); there is insufficient evidence for long-term benefits and practice has outpaced understanding ([Dichev & Dicheva 2017](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-017-0042-5)); theoretical grounding is uneven ([Seaborn & Fels 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2014.09.006); [Hamari et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1109/hicss.2014.377)); the health evidence base is positive but limited in quality ([Johnson et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.invent.2016.10.002)); and the app-feature-to-SDT mapping exercise analyses 208 apps analytically without measuring need satisfaction in any user ([Villalobos-Zúñiga & Cherubini 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2020.102449)). On ADHD, the one paper connecting SDT to the disorder argues that ADHD research has focused on reinforcement effects while neglecting internal motives and proposes SDT as a guiding framework, while raising the potential negative effects of external reinforcers on intrinsic motivation and wellbeing as an open research question ([Morsink et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/10870547211050948)). It is a position paper, not evidence.

**Weakens the design.** Three concessions. Autonomy is the weakest leg: claiming the pet supports autonomy claims the one thing the key experiment failed to produce. The paper that best licenses an SDT framing for ADHD explicitly flags external reinforcers as a risk to intrinsic motivation — a caution aimed at treats and decorations however warmly framed. And the incoherence finding means "designed using SDT" invites scrutiny rather than settling anything: the framing is defensible only if your evaluation *measures* autonomy, competence and relatedness with a validated need-satisfaction scale. There is no retrieved evidence that a virtual pet satisfies relatedness in adults with ADHD over time; the nearest support is a short general-population study of avatars and teammates.

## Group C — claims to verify or discard

### C1 Urgency-driven initiation

**Conclusion.** The community claim is a garbled folk version of a real finding — acute uncontrollable stress does rapidly weaken prefrontal function while strengthening amygdala and striatal circuits, but that literature concerns stress rather than deadline urgency, is largely animal and general-population rather than ADHD, and describes impairment rather than productive initiation; the defensible ADHD account of urgency is arousal and state regulation plus reinforcement salience and delay aversion.

**Evidence strength.** Observational and mechanistic review for the source claim (animal-dominant); meta-analysis for the replacement account.

Here is where the claim comes from. Acute uncontrollable stress increases catecholamine release in prefrontal cortex, reducing neuronal firing through α1 and D1 feedforward signalling that opens potassium channels and weakens synaptic efficacy in spines, while high catecholamines strengthen the primary sensory cortices, amygdala and striatum, rapidly flipping the brain from reflective to reflexive control of behaviour ([Arnsten 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4087), *Nature Neuroscience*; the case for PFC's selective stress vulnerability in [Arnsten 2009](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn2648)). The human counterpart is a pharmacological fMRI study in which coupling increased within a salience network including amygdala, thalamus, hypothalamus, midbrain and frontoinsular/dACC in proportion to stress-response magnitude, abolished by β-adrenergic blockade but not by cortisol synthesis inhibition ([Hermans et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1209603), *Science*).

Four things are wrong with the community version. The trigger in this literature is acute uncontrollable stress or threat, not an approaching deadline on a task you intend to do. The consequence described is *impaired* higher cognition and a switch to habitual control — a deficit account that cannot by itself explain improved initiation. The shift is toward a distributed salience network including striatum and sensory cortex, not a handover of "drive" to the amygdala as a single locus. And it rests mainly on rodent and non-human-primate work plus healthy humans, while the same author's ADHD review argues the opposite direction for ADHD: prefrontal regions are *under*-active with weakened connectivity ([Arnsten 2009](https://doi.org/10.2165/00023210-200923000-00005), *CNS Drugs*), and catecholamine modulation of dlPFC follows an inverted U with all effective ADHD medications acting by enhancing that transmission ([Arnsten 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2011.01.027)). The exploitable tension is that if ADHD involves too little effective catecholamine signalling in PFC, raising arousal moves a person *up* the inverted U toward better function, whereas the stress literature describes going far past the optimum. Urgency-as-helpful and stress-as-impairing are two different points on one curve, and the claim conflates them.

The defensible replacement is state regulation plus reinforcement salience. Event rate systematically moderates go/no-go performance in ADHD, with performance worst under slow, under-stimulating conditions ([Metin et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2012.08.023), *Biological Psychiatry*) — the state-regulation prediction confirmed meta-analytically. The state-regulation deficit is formally distinguished from delay aversion ([Sonuga-Barke et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11065-009-9115-0), *Neuropsychology Review*), ADHD performance is unusually sensitive to reinforcement contingencies ([Luman et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2004.11.001)), and delay aversion supplies the reason a distant deadline is aversive to contemplate while a near one becomes tractable ([Sonuga-Barke 2002](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0166-4328%2801%2900432-6)). A defensible sentence for the dissertation: *urgency plausibly aids initiation in ADHD by raising arousal toward an optimum on an inverted-U dose-response curve for catecholamine signalling in prefrontal cortex, and by converting a temporally distant, delay-aversive consequence into an immediate and salient one — not by transferring control from prefrontal cortex to amygdala.*

**Contradicts the design.** This removes a justification, then creates a new problem. If the report uses the PFC-to-amygdala story anywhere, even as motivation, a marker who checks Arnsten will find a mechanism describing impairment under acute uncontrollable stress in animals. Worse, the *correct* account is about arousal and salience, and a single daily notification with randomised copy, ±10 min jitter and exponential back-off is a deliberately low-arousal, low-salience, highly predictable stimulus, while the event-rate meta-analysis shows ADHD performance degrades under slow, under-stimulating conditions. Your answer has to be that warmth-based social reinforcement ([Kohls et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1186/1744-9081-5-20)) substitutes for arousal, stated explicitly rather than letting the urgency literature appear to be support.

### C2 Heterogeneity and the 5–10% transfer figure

**Conclusion.** No published 5–10% figure for single-mechanism ADHD intervention transfer exists — no credible evidence found, but the heterogeneity claim behind it is strongly supported, and its defensible quantitative form is per-deficit prevalence within diagnosed samples, roughly 18–62% per mechanism rather than 5–10%.

**Evidence strength.** None found for the figure; meta-analysis and case-control observational for heterogeneity.

Searches in both OpenAlex and PubMed across several phrasings found no record that states, models or estimates a 5–10% transfer rate for a mechanism-targeted ADHD intervention. Attribute it to your practitioner source as expert opinion, or drop it.

The claim behind it is well supported. Across 83 studies, EF effect sizes fall in the medium range (0.46–0.69) and the lack of universality of EF deficits means EF weaknesses are neither necessary nor sufficient to cause all cases of ADHD ([Willcutt et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2005.02.006)), and group-mean comparisons conceal distributional overlap so that only a subgroup has a deficit in any given mechanism ([Nigg et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2004.08.025)). The papers that put fractions on this are what you actually need. Assessing 83 medication-naive boys against 66 controls across six confirmed domains, the percentage with a deficit was: working memory 30.1%, inhibition 22.9%, delay aversion 36.1%, decision making 20.5%, timing 31.3%, response variability 18.1% — a minority in every domain ([Coghill et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0033291713002547), *Psychological Medicine*). The necessary counterweight: with a multi-test battery correcting for task unreliability, 89% of 136 children with ADHD were impaired on at least one EF, though per-function fractions remained partial at 62% working memory, 27% inhibitory control and 38% set shifting ([Kofler et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10802-018-0438-2)). In adults, effect sizes were small to moderate (0.05–0.70) with 11% of 133 diagnosed adults showing no neuropsychological dysfunction at all ([Mostert et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euroneuro.2015.08.010)).

The mechanism-level version is the multiple-pathway work: inhibitory, temporal-processing and delay-related deficits co-occurred no more than chance, with substantial groups showing only one problem ([Sonuga-Barke et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2009.12.018)); EF, reaction-time variability and emotional functioning each contributed independently ([Sjöwall et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12006)); three latent classes covered 87% of an ADHD sample but two resembled subgroups among typically developing controls ([van Hulst et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0033291714001792)), echoing the finding that some heterogeneity is nested in normal variation ([Fair et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1115365109)). State the replication problems: five neuropsychological latent classes replicated across twin datasets and were heritable, but less so than ADHD status itself and not strong candidate endophenotypes ([Arnett et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/10870547221075842)). **Retraction warning:** the best-known temperament-subtyping paper carries `is_retracted = true` in both OpenAlex and CrossRef, with a 2018 Notice of Retraction and Replacement ([Karalunas et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2014.763); notice at [10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2018.0013](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2018.0013)) — cite [Karalunas & Nigg 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2019.11.002) or [Nigg et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bpsc.2020.02.005) instead. At the individual level, deviations from a normative brain model overlapped between any two adults with persistent ADHD in only a few loci, so the average patient has limited informative value ([Wolfers et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0033291719000084)).

A defensible substitute for the 5–10% sentence: *ADHD is defined symptomatically rather than aetiologically, and no single neurocognitive mechanism is impaired in more than a minority of diagnosed individuals — across six domains in a medication-naive childhood sample the proportion with a deficit ranged from 18.1% to 36.1% ([Coghill et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0033291713002547)), and even under a reliability-corrected multi-test battery only 27–62% were impaired on any specific executive function ([Kofler et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10802-018-0438-2)). An intervention operating through one mechanism should therefore be expected to be relevant to a minority of users, and a small evaluation should be read for divergent individual outcomes rather than a group mean.* Two caveats to state: these are deficit prevalences, not intervention-response rates, an inference the papers do not make; and the arithmetic is measurement-dependent, as the 89%-on-at-least-one-EF result shows.

**Weakens the design.** Three ways. It undercuts the thesis as a population-level claim — if a reminder mechanism reaches only a subgroup, so does an anchor, and the dissociation result means the pet cannot be assumed to be the operative mechanism for a given user; restate as "for some users, an external anchor may initiate action." It weakens the case for one fixed feature set, since a single interaction model is a single-mechanism intervention by construction and the "average user" has limited informative value. And it removes the quantitative cover for the small evaluation: with no published figure, heterogeneity means a small-*N* study cannot distinguish "the mechanism does not work" from "this sample did not contain the responsive subgroup."

### C3 Status of Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria

**Conclusion.** The r/ADHD moderation position is substantially accurate about the *term* — RSD is absent from DSM-5-TR and ICD-11, has no validated measure, criteria or prevalence estimate, and originates in non-peer-reviewed clinical and consumer writing, but that finding cannot be extended to the underlying experience, which is captured by two well-evidenced constructs from different traditions: emotional dysregulation in ADHD and rejection sensitivity.

**Evidence strength.** None found for RSD as a validated construct; meta-analysis for emotional dysregulation and for rejection sensitivity.

Searching the term is the decisive test. A PubMed exact-phrase search returns 14 records, most concerning *gender* dysphoria or using the phrase only in passing; OpenAlex title searches return 18 and 15 works whose composition is itself the finding — Zenodo and OSF preprints, a trade newsletter, book chapters, a celebrity news item. There is no validation study, no psychometric instrument and no epidemiological estimate for RSD anywhere in that set. The peer-reviewed items that exist are critiques. A Dutch-language critical reflection states that William Dodson introduced the concept from clinical experience with ADHD clients and that it spread through social media, identifying four risks in clinical use including terminological fragmentation and limited empirical evidence, and recommending the established rejection-sensitivity spectrum instead (van Asselt 2026, *Tijdschrift voor Psychiatrie* 68(3):127–130; no DOI in the retrieved record — [PMID 41944472](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41944472/)). The same author's scoping review screened 1,285 titles and 37 full texts, found only 12 eligible studies, and reported that conceptualisations and operationalisations varied considerably, that no study examined responses to criticism or evaluated interventions, and that quantitative findings on intensity were inconsistent ([van Asselt et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/27546330261441753), *Neurodiversity*); another paper states directly that there is no consensus in definition or application across medical, academic and informal environments ([Sandland 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/27546330251394516)). The term's originator appears in the indexed peer-reviewed literature essentially only in a commentary questioning a treatment practice ([Dodson & Modestino 2024](https://doi.org/10.2174/0122115560297397240404071044)). On classification: the ICD-11 ADHD entry (6A05, with presentations 6A05.0/.1/.2/.Y/.Z) defines only inattention and hyperactivity-impulsivity, with no rejection-related construct — cite the classification systems directly rather than secondary clinical pages.

What the moderation statement gets wrong if read too broadly is the phenomenology. Emotional dysregulation in ADHD is among the better-evidenced non-core features in the field: across 13 studies of clinically diagnosed *adults* versus controls (*N* = 2,535), general emotion dysregulation was Hedges' *g* = 1.17 (*p* < 0.001), emotional lability the strongest facet at *g* = 1.20 [0.57, 1.83], with *r* = 0.54 between symptom severity and dysregulation ([Beheshti et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-020-2442-7), *BMC Psychiatry*). Emotion dysregulation is prevalent across the lifespan and a major contributor to impairment, though whether it is a core or associated feature is genuinely contested — three competing models are set out and you should say so rather than pick one ([Shaw et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2013.13070966), *American Journal of Psychiatry*). Barkley's deficient emotional self-regulation is operationalised in 206 adults with ADHD, 55% of whom reported extreme DESR exceeding that of 95% of 123 controls, with associated impairment in quality of life, marital status, traffic accidents and arrests ([Surman et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12402-012-0100-8)); in children, emotional lability ratings ran more than 1.5 SD above normative means in 1,186 cases ([Sobanski et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7610.2010.02217.x)).

Rejection sensitivity is a construct from a different tradition — social and personality psychology — introduced as a cognitive-affective disposition to anxiously expect, readily perceive and overreact to rejection, with its own measure, in general-population adults with no ADHD involvement ([Downey & Feldman 1996](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.70.6.1327)), and with a meta-analytic evidence base linking it to mental-health outcomes ([Gao et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2017.08.007)). The bridge between the traditions is thin but real, and its best study is unusually close to your users: 1,235 German 10–19-year-olds, symptom-defined and *not* diagnosed, where those with ADHD symptoms reported significantly higher anxious and angry rejection sensitivity, which partially mediated the link to comorbid problems ([Bondü & Esser 2015](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00787-014-0560-9)). The remaining ADHD-and-rejection evidence is qualitative or paediatric: a five-undergraduate focus group ([Rowney-Smith et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0314669)); 43 diagnosed adults who spontaneously described emotional dysregulation "including rejection-sensitive dysphoria," which evidences the folk term's active use rather than its validity ([Ginapp et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0292721)); criticism experiences in 162 adults with ADHD and high ADHD traits ([Beaton et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0263366)); and on actual peer rejection in children, sociometric evidence of being less liked with fewer friends ([Hoza et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-006x.73.3.411)) and a 109-study meta-analysis (*n* = 104,813) finding peer functioning the most impaired domain at *r* = .33 ([Ros & Graziano 2018](https://doi.org/10.1080/15374416.2016.1266644)).

**Contradicts the design.** The first consequence is significant and localises your counter-risk in a specific decision — not the warmth-only feedback, but *silence on lapse combined with exponential back-off*. A *g* = 1.17 dysregulation effect in diagnosed adults and 55% showing DESR beyond the 95th percentile of controls describe users for whom a perceived reproach is not a neutral signal, and the established construct is precisely the disposition to readily perceive intentional rejection in ambiguous behaviour. Silence and progressive withdrawal of contact are ambiguous by construction and readily read as rejection *by the pet*. Absence of negative pet reactions is not absence of perceived rejection; your chosen failure mode may be the guilt-debt pathway rather than the protection against it. Probe this directly in evaluation — ask users what the pet's silence meant to them. The second consequence is terminological: using "RSD" as a mechanism or user characteristic imports a term with no validated measure and no criteria, and a reviewer will flag it. Name the construct you mean, note that the folk term is widely used in the communities the app targets, and keep the two claims separate.

### C4 Retention benchmarks

**Conclusion.** Against real-world commercial data, ">50% still using it at two weeks" is extraordinarily demanding — roughly an order of magnitude above the median mental-health app, whose 15-day retention is about 4%, but against a recruited student trial it is unremarkable, and the offline architecture means you cannot measure it the way any of these papers did.

**Evidence strength.** Observational panel data for the benchmark; meta-analysis for trial attrition.

The anchor figure comes from 93 Android apps for anxiety, depression or wellbeing with 10,000+ installs (median 100,000), measured through an independent usage panel rather than developer self-report: median daily-active-user open rate 4.0% (IQR 4.7%), median 15-day retention 3.9% (IQR 10.3%) and 30-day retention 3.3% (IQR 6.2%), with tracker apps — the closest category to PetTodo — at median 6.1% (IQR 20.4%) on day 30 ([Baumel et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.2196/14567), *JMIR*). Read precisely, that is retention of *installers* in an open cohort who found the app organically with no recruitment or assessment contact, so >50% against that baseline is not demanding but wildly optimistic.

A student trial will look far better, and the size of that gap is quantified: across 11 comparisons with proactive recruitment and pre-post assessment, trial-reported usage was a median 4.06 times higher (IQR 4.49) than real-world usage of the same programs, while the three papers without proactive procedures gave ratios of 0.84, 1.05 and 1.27 ([Baumel et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/tbm/ibz147), *Translational Behavioral Medicine*). Trial attrition itself runs 24.1% short-term rising to 35.5% longer-term across 70 RCTs ([Linardon & Fuller-Tyszkiewicz 2020](https://doi.org/10.1037/ccp0000459)) and a pooled 26.2% rising to 47.8% after adjustment for publication bias across 18 RCTs ([Torous et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2019.11.167)), against the canonical law-of-attrition framing ([Eysenbach 2005](https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.7.1.e11)). So roughly 75% two-week retention is normal for a supervised trial cohort and >50% is a soft target. Two figures not to misuse: expert-rated app quality correlates with downloads and monthly active users but shows extremely small correlations with 1-, 7- and 30-day retention ([Kaveladze et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.2196/30766)); and mean adherence of 56.0% (SD 24.4%) is the ratio of actual to intended use within studies, not install-cohort survival ([Jakob et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.2196/35371)). Note also that across 92 RCTs apps improved outcomes (*g* = 0.43) while persuasive design predicted neither efficacy nor engagement, with 25 distinct engagement metrics in use and 24% of studies reporting none ([Valentine et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-025-01567-5)); the closest setting match for a student-recruited evaluation gives small effects with attrition reported ([Jabs et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/eip.70207); framing in [Lipschitz et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40501-023-00297-3)).

**Contradicts the design.** This is primarily a measurement problem and it bites hard. With all data on-device and no server, you cannot compute retention the way any cited paper does: those figures come from a cross-app usage panel, commercial retention estimates, or trial records. Your options are self-report at follow-up (inflated roughly fourfold on the evidence above), a manual export the participant returns, or in-person device checks — each changes what "retention" means and none is comparable to the 3.9% benchmark. Reframe the users' standard explicitly as a small-cohort *trial*-retention target, reported alongside the inflation factor. And note that two weeks is short enough that even a clean 50%+ result says nothing about the design thesis, since the dependency effect, the day-30 collapse and post-incentive decay all sit after the window the evaluation can see.

### C5 Child-to-adult transfer and time anchors

**Conclusion.** Child-to-adult transfer is poor and asymmetric — the child evidence base is dominated by parent- and teacher-mediated interventions with no adult analogue, non-pharmacological effects in both age groups collapse when outcome assessment is blinded, and adult guidelines recommend a different modality; and event-based cues are reliably easier than time-based cues for ADHD samples including adults, so an event anchor is better grounded than a countdown, but no study compares concrete against abstract time framing, and no credible evidence was found that timers cause stress.

**Evidence strength.** Meta-analysis for transfer and for timing deficits; experimental for the event-versus-time dissociation; none found for concrete-versus-abstract framing and none for timer-induced anxiety.

The decisive result on transfer is a matched blinding finding across age groups. Meta-analysing 54 of 2,904 screened records, all dietary (SMD 0.21–0.48) and psychological (SMD 0.40–0.64) treatments were significant with raters closest to the therapeutic setting, but under best probably-blinded assessment only free fatty acids (0.16) and artificial food colour exclusion (0.42) survived, with cognitive training, neurofeedback and behavioural interventions substantially attenuated to non-significant levels ([Sonuga-Barke et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2012.12070991), *American Journal of Psychiatry*). In adults the mirror image appears: across 113 RCTs and 14,887 formally diagnosed adults, atomoxetine (−0.38 self, −0.51 clinician) and stimulants beat placebo on both scales, while CBT (−0.76), cognitive remediation (−1.35), mindfulness (−0.79), psychoeducation (−0.77) and tDCS (−0.78) beat placebo *only* on clinician-reported measures ([Ostinelli et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/s2215-0366%2824%2900360-2), *Lancet Psychiatry*). Content differs by age rather than transferring: GRADE recommendations by age band support CBT for adults and caregiver intervention for younger populations — structurally different interventions, one of which cannot exist for an adult living alone ([Tourjman et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci12081023)), with adult-specific CBT synthesis in Cochrane ([López et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.cd010840.pub2)) and separate adult guidance for the same reason ([Kooij et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eurpsy.2018.11.001)). The canonical child cognitive-training transfer failure is [Rapport et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2013.08.005), and age moderates the correlates of the very mechanism you rely on: across 824 effect sizes the time-perception deficit averages *g* = 0.688, but the working-memory link applied only below 18 while ADHD-subtype effects applied only at 18 and over ([Metcalfe et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1080/87565641.2023.2293712)).

On time anchors, three meta-analyses establish that "how long is left" is a measurably unreliable internal quantity ([Metcalfe et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1080/87565641.2023.2293712); [Zheng et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054720978557); [Marx et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2021.12.004)) — an argument for externalising time and simultaneously against an abstract countdown that asks the user to convert a number into a degraded internal sense of duration. The relevant finding is the event-versus-time dissociation, and it holds in adults: on the Dresden Breakfast Task with parallel constraints, 25 adults with ADHD showed no group difference in event-based prospective memory but a large impairment in time-based PM ([Altgassen et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054712445484)), with the same pattern across six child studies ([Talbot et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1080/13854046.2017.1393563)) and an ordering in which activity-based cues beat event-based, which beat time-based ([Yang et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/87565641.2019.1695801)) — suggesting anchors tied to the user's *own* ongoing activity may beat anchors tied to another person's. Task planning is separately impaired in adults ([Fuermaier et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0058338)). Offloading supports externalising the cue ([Gilbert et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-022-02139-4)), and reminders improved accuracy for time-based intentions in healthy adults, with offloading rising with delay and driven partly by metacognitive belief rather than actual need, but at 10–30-second laboratory timescales ([Tsai & Gilbert 2025](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-025-01708-x)). The only relevant time-aid trial is a randomised block/waiting-list design with 47 children aged 6–11 across mixed diagnoses (37 analysed), where the intervention group improved time-processing ability more than controls ([Janeslätt et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.3109/11038128.2013.870225)).

Two explicit nulls. On abstract timers causing stress or anxiety: no credible evidence found, having searched time pressure and evaluation apprehension, test anxiety under time limits, countdown timers and stress, and deadline proximity across OpenAlex and PubMed. Do not assert it. On concrete or relative versus abstract temporal framing for initiation: no credible evidence found.

**Contradicts the design.** Every mechanism PetTodo borrows is child-evidenced — warmth-only reinforcement, the animal anchor, if-then planning, the event-rate result, and the transfer literature says that is unsafe; the design cannot claim adult evidence for any of them. The blinding result is a direct threat to your evaluation: behavioural-intervention effects vanished under blinded assessment in children and psychological interventions beat placebo only on clinician-rated outcomes in adults, so an unblinded self-report evaluation of a warm, appealing app that users chose to install is measuring the most inflation-prone outcome in the field. Pre-register an objective behavioural outcome — logged completions against a no-pet control period. Guideline-level evidence also points adults toward CBT-type skills work, and PetTodo omits all planning, prioritisation, breakdown and self-monitoring: a defensible scope decision, but state it as a *deviation* from the adult evidence base rather than an improvement on it. On the anchor itself, the support is for cue *type*, not for pet voice and not for anchoring to another person's activity — if the copy references a household member's activity the user must still monitor an external event they do not control, which is closer to a time-based cue than the design assumes. And randomised copy with jitter deliberately varies the cue, in tension with the cue distinctiveness that makes event-based PM work; nothing retrieved says this is harmful, but nothing says it helps.

### C6 Personal avatars and uncanny renderings

**Conclusion.** Self-relevant and customised avatars reliably increase identification and produce small-to-medium behavioural effects with at least one clear null, and an uncanny valley for virtual animals is empirically demonstrated and strongest for morbid imagery, but there is no credible evidence on rendering the user's own pet versus a generic one, nor on distress responses to a rendering of a deceased pet.

**Evidence strength.** Meta-analysis for the personalisation effect; observational rating studies for the animal uncanny valley; none found for the own-pet comparison and none for the deceased-pet case.

The Proteus effect across 46 randomised experiments is .22–.26, explicitly small-to-medium ([Ratan et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1080/15213269.2019.1623698), *Media Psychology*). Randomising 305 adults to motion-capture avatars showing their own or a stranger's face while running, own-face avatars raised cardiac frequency ([Navarro et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1080/10410236.2020.1834194)), but the outcome is physiological intensity, not sustained engagement or task completion. The key counter-evidence: comparing photorealistic own-self against friend avatars in a search-and-rescue game, there was no effect of avatar appearance on performance or subjective experience ([Wauck et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1145/3173574.3174059), CHI). Identification does increase autonomy, invested effort and time played ([Birk et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1145/2858036.2858062)), customisation increases engagement in a digital-health framing ([Birk & Mandryk 2019](https://doi.org/10.2196/10133)) and identification and empathy toward the character ([Türkay & Kinzer 2014](https://doi.org/10.4018/ijgcms.2014010101)), and functions as a self-affirmation task reducing defensive processing of threatening health information ([Kang & Kim 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2020.106446)), with tailoring supported generally across 88 computer-tailored interventions ([Krebs et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ypmed.2010.06.004)). Crucially, all of this concerns an avatar that *is* the user, whereas PetTodo's pet is a separate being the user cares for — the self-relevance mechanism may not transfer, and no study compares an own-pet rendering against a generic pet.

An uncanny valley for virtual animals is confirmed in familiarity and commonality for both still and moving images across 163 participants on four continents, and is reported as particularly pronounced for morbid images ([Sierra Rátiva et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/cav.2043)), with independent establishment of the animal case ([Schwind et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2017.11.003)). Review evidence, however, finds no consistent support for the naive uncanny valley or movement modulation and good support for perceptual mismatch, concluding the valley exists only under specific conditions ([Kätsyri et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00390)), so a clearly stylised rendering may sit outside it.

On bereavement, among 71 owners bereaved within a year, prior attachment strength and attachment anxiety each uniquely predicted grief severity, with the continuing bond partially mediating ([Field et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1080/07481180802705783)), and a PRISMA synthesis found continuing bonds can aggravate and intensify grief where it is disenfranchised, though appropriate bonds can moderate it ([Hughes & Harkin 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/00302228221125955); see also [Park et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/10888705.2021.1934839); [Cordaro 2012](https://doi.org/10.17744/mehc.34.4.41q0248450t98072)). The deceased-recreation literature is normative rather than empirical and concerns human deceased ([Hollanek & Nowaczyk-Basińska 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-024-00744-w); [Lindemann 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11948-022-00417-x); [Öhman & Floridi 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-018-0335-2)), with empirical work finding that technologies designed for interacting with the dead have largely failed while users repurpose ordinary communication tools instead ([Morse 2024](https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118x.2023.2205467)).

**Contradicts the design.** Generating the pet from the user's own photograph *as the core engagement mechanism* over-claims on current evidence: the meta-analytic effect is small-to-medium, photorealistic self-similarity produced no measurable benefit in one CHI experiment, and no study compares an own-pet rendering against a generic pet. More actionable: the design has no stated handling of the deceased-pet case, and the animal uncanny valley being most pronounced for morbid images means your rendering pipeline's failure modes — sick-looking, malformed or otherwise "off" output — fall exactly where negative affect toward animal characters is strongest, landing on a user whose grief severity is predicted by the strength of the very attachment the design recruits. There is no evidence either way on net harm, and that absence is the finding. Evidence-supported mitigations: keep the rendering clearly stylised rather than near-photorealistic, guard against morbid rendering artefacts, and treat "is this animal still alive?" as a design question requiring an explicit answer.

## Where nothing was found

These are genuine absences after targeted searching, not gaps in effort, and several are the most useful results in the review. No study measures guilt toward a neglected virtual pet, or tests a warmth-only anthropomorphic character against a punitive one. No virtual-pet or care-taking efficacy evidence exists for adults with ADHD, diagnosed or self-identified. No study compares external anchors against reminder- or planning-based interventions on task initiation in any population; none tests body doubling with a non-human agent; none examines pet ownership and task initiation in ADHD adults. No overjustification or reward-withdrawal study has been run in an ADHD sample, and no published work documents users falsifying task completions to obtain in-app rewards. No meta-analysis of prospective memory in ADHD exists at any age, and no time-perception meta-analysis is restricted to adults. No study manipulates the number of visible items in a task list and measures avoidance or completion, and no choice-overload study uses an ADHD sample. No empirical work exists on small random timing offsets, or on exponential back-off of prompts after non-engagement. No study measures need satisfaction from a virtual pet in adults with ADHD. No published 5–10% single-mechanism transfer figure exists, and no validated measure, criteria or prevalence estimate exists for Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria. No evidence was found that countdown timers cause stress or anxiety, or on concrete versus abstract temporal framing for initiation. And nothing compares a rendering of the user's own animal against a generic character, or examines responses to a digital recreation of a deceased pet.
