## Report structure (from the supervisor, Dr Mary McGee Wood)

Two emails, the second correcting the first. Net result — **six sections, no
separate Background**:

- **Abstract** — no more than half a page
- **1. Introduction** — brief context and motivation, aims and objectives, and
  **the concise literature review** (the project team's guidance moved it here;
  the supervisor's original email had a separate Background section and they
  retracted it). At least two key references.
- **2. Methodology** — the methods used, with **critical justification for why
  these rather than other possible ones**. Technical background to the
  implementation.
- **3. Implementation** — what was actually built to solve the problem.
- **4. Results** — lots of screenshots, each *explained*, not just included.
- **5. Testing and evaluation** — software testing **and user testing**.
- **6. Conclusion and future work**

The supervisor also warned against using the marking rubric's structure as the
report outline — that is what this structure replaces.

## Supervisor-side signal (2026-08-13)

The supervisor forwarded feedback from a specialist in early years special needs
education, who rated the ADHD/focus-pet space highly and suggested combining a
virtual pet with a to-do list — ticking things off within a set time to earn the
pet treats, or gating the fun part of the game while a work timer runs. They also
noted that some children find timers stressful in the abstract but respond well
to a *concrete relative* anchor ("let's see if we can finish this before they
finish making your lunch"). Reference product they like:
`https://www.yourfocusfriend.com/`.

This is a credible external voice for section 5's evaluation, and the relative
time anchor is a design idea that fits the ADHD-first gate. Not yet decided.
